Example · synthetic-aperture radar

$\delta_1$ for synthetic-aperture radar

Same framework — different instrument. The optical pupil is replaced by a time-frequency waveform; the image plane becomes $(\text{range},\,\text{cross-range})$. The matched-filter contest, the sign test $\bar{\mathcal{D}} \gtrless 0$, and the resolution metric $\delta_1$ all carry over verbatim — we only swap the unit instrument response function (UIRF).

Drinor Cacaj — Cacaj et al. 2025, A confusion framework for direct imaging.

The model

A SAR platform moves along the along-track $x$-axis at altitude $H$, transmitting pulses of waveform $s(t)$. Each ground target $i$ at slant range $r_i$ and cross-range $x_i$ produces a return that depends on the instantaneous two-way range $R_i(x_p) = \sqrt{r_i^2 + (x_p - x_i)^2} \approx r_i + (x_p - x_i)^2 / (2\,r_i)$. For $n$ targets:

$$ y(t, x_p) \;=\; \sum_i \alpha_i\, s\!\bigl(t - 2 R_i(x_p)/c\bigr)\, e^{-j 4\pi R_i(x_p) / \lambda} \;+\; n(t, x_p), $$

with $\alpha_i \geq 0$ the (intensity-equivalent) reflectivity and $n$ additive Gaussian receiver noise. Two-step matched filtering — range compression on fast time, then azimuth compression on slow time — collapses each return to a separable 2D impulse response on the image plane $(r, x)$:

$$ \mu_P(r, x) \;\approx\; \sum_i \alpha_i\, h_r(r - r_i)\, h_x(x - x_i), \qquad U_{p_i}(r, x) \;:=\; h_r(r - r_i)\, h_x(x - x_i). $$

This is exactly the framework's $\mu_P = \sum F_i\, U_{p_i}$ with $F_i = \alpha_i$ and the SAR impulse response $U_p$ in place of the optical PSF — so cost $J$, $Q^\star$, $\bar{\mathcal{D}}$ and $\delta_1$ all carry over.

The range axis

The range PSF $h_r$ is the autocorrelation of the transmit waveform. For a rectangular pulse of duration $T$ (bandwidth $B \approx 1/T$),

$$ h_r(\tau) \;=\; T\,\mathrm{tri}\!\bigl(\tau/T\bigr), \qquad \delta_r \;\approx\; \frac{c\,T}{2} \;=\; \frac{c}{2B}, $$

a triangular envelope of half-width $T$. Pulse-compressed waveforms — LFM chirp, NLFM with apodization, polyphase codes — decouple bandwidth from duration: an LFM of duration $T$ and independent bandwidth $B \gg 1/T$ gives $h_r(\tau) \approx \mathrm{sinc}(B\tau)$ with the same $\delta_r = c/(2B)$ but $BT$ times more energy on target. The waveform plays exactly the role the pupil mask plays in the optical demo — and like a pupil, its shape (apodization) trades main-lobe width against sidelobe height.

The cross-range axis

For a target at slant range $r_0$, the platform's along-track motion across an integration interval of length $L_{\rm syn}$ illuminates the target through a "synthetic aperture" whose width is $L_{\rm syn}$. By the standard Fourier-aperture argument (echoing $\lambda/d$ for an optical pupil), the cross-range PSF has width

$$ h_x(x) \;\propto\; \mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{2 L_{\rm syn}\, x}{\lambda\, r_0}\right), \qquad \delta_x \;\approx\; \frac{\lambda\, r_0}{2\, L_{\rm syn}}. $$

As $L_{\rm syn}$ grows, $\delta_x$ shrinks — the same "longer baseline → narrower beam" trade as a real-aperture telescope. A synthetic aperture of $L_{\rm syn} = 100$ m at $\lambda = 3$ cm and $r_0 = 800$ km gives $\delta_x \approx 12$ cm — orders of magnitude better than any real antenna of comparable size.

Two-target confusion

For two equal-flux targets at $(\tilde r_1, \tilde x_1)$ and $(\tilde r_2, \tilde x_2)$, the mean SAR image is $\mu_P = U_{p_1} + U_{p_2}$ — the superposition of two shifted 2D impulse responses. As the separation shrinks below the joint resolution cell, the matched-filter $Q^\star$ ceases to tell them apart and the contamination criterion $\bar{\mathcal{D}} > 0$ activates. This is identical to the optical demo's "two sources blur into one" except the image plane is $(\tilde r, \tilde x)$ instead of $(\theta_x, \theta_y)$.

The framework, applied

The matched-filter cost $J(P, Q) = \|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|^2_{\Sigma^{-1}}$, the best one-source explanation $Q^\star$, the AIC parsimony cost $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}^2$, and the three contamination conditions (detect / resolve fails / contaminate) all carry over without modification. The reduction to a single sign test

$$ \bar{\mathcal{D}}(P) \;\gtrless\; 0 \qquad\text{(contamination possible $\Leftrightarrow$ $\bar{\mathcal{D}} > 0$)} $$

is exactly the same scalar quantity — it depends only on the PSF family and the flux ratio, never on the noise level. The resolution

$$ \delta_1[\text{SAR}] \;=\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{\pi}\, \mathrm{area}\{p_2 \in \mathrm{FOV} : \bar{\mathcal{D}}(\{p_1, p_2\}) > 0\}} $$

(measured in resolution cells once the axes are normalized) collapses the full geometry to one number per waveform / aperture choice — directly comparable across SAR configurations, and to the optical $\delta_1 \approx 1.16\,\lambda/d$ of a disk pupil. $\delta_1 \,=\, \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{\pi}\,\mathrm{area}\{\bar{\mathcal{D}}>0\}} \;\approx\;$ —

In normalized resolution-cell units, a single-pulse SAR with rectangular waveform and uniform synthetic aperture gives $\delta_1 \approx 0.95$ cells (matching the disk's $0.952\,\delta_{\mathrm{Rayleigh}}$ to 3 decimals — same kernel structure). LFM and NLFM modify the range axis only, shifting $\delta_1$ via sidelobe suppression on one principal axis. A longer $L_{\rm syn}$ narrows the cross-range cell — the $\delta_1$ in cells is invariant, but the physical $\delta_x$ in metres shrinks linearly with $L_{\rm syn}$.

The point: $\delta_1$ as defined in the main framework is a single number that ranks any SAR configuration on the same scale as a coronagraph or a nuller. Plug in the UIRF; everything else is machinery.
