A confusion framework for direct imaging

When a telescope's PSF is wide enough that two faint sources blur into one, no algorithm can reliably separate them. This framework turns that intuition into a single sharp number — the target confusion map $\mathcal{D}$ — and makes the threshold computable for every PSF you'd actually use.

Drinor Cacaj — Cacaj et al. 2025, A confusion framework for direct imaging.

The model

Fix a field of view $\Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ and a unit-norm shift-invariant PSF dictionary $\{U_\theta\}_{\theta \in \Theta}$. A configuration of point sources is a finite set

$$ P \;=\; \{(p_i, F_i)\}_{i=1}^n, \qquad p_i \in \Theta, \ F_i > 0. $$

The detector mean is the flux-weighted superposition of shifted PSFs,

$$ \mu_P \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^n F_i\, U_{p_i}, $$

and the observed data is $\mu_P$ corrupted by additive Gaussian noise with covariance $\Sigma$ (typically $\Sigma = \sigma^2 I$, per-pixel detector noise).

The cost function

The natural cost between two configurations $P, Q$ is the squared Mahalanobis distance between their means:

$$ J(P, Q) \;=\; \|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}^2 \;=\; (\mu_P - \mu_Q)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (\mu_P - \mu_Q). $$

It is symmetric, non-negative, and zero only when $\mu_P = \mu_Q$. Up to an additive constant, $J$ is the symmetrised KL divergence between the Gaussian likelihoods generated by $P$ and $Q$ — so a likelihood ratio test on the data reduces exactly to a comparison of $J$ values.

The best one-source explanation $Q^\star$

Let $\mathcal{Q}_1 = \{(q, F_q) : q \in \Theta,\ F_q \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}$ be the set of single-source configurations. The best one-source explanation of $P$ is

$$ Q^\star \;=\; \arg\min_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}_1} J(P, Q). $$

For any fixed candidate position $q$, the optimal flux $F_q^\star$ is analytic — it is the matched-filter coefficient $F_q^\star = \langle U_q,\, \mu_P\rangle_{\Sigma^{-1}}$ — so the $Q^\star$ search reduces to a 1D maximisation of $f(q) = \langle U_q,\, \mu_P\rangle_{\Sigma^{-1}}^2$ over $q \in \Theta$.

A parsimony cost for model selection (AIC)

Comparing models of different cardinality requires a parsimony cost. The total cost of a candidate model $Q$ is

$$ J_{\mathrm{tot}}(P, Q) \;=\; J(P, Q) \;+\; \xi(|Q|), $$

with $\xi : \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ strictly increasing and $\xi(0) = 0$. Under the AIC scaling $\xi(N) = N \eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}^2$, adding any single source costs the fixed penalty

$$ \eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}^2 \;:=\; \xi(N + 1) - \xi(N), $$

and $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}$ becomes both the detection threshold (below which a configuration is undetectable) and the indistinguishability tolerance.

The natural unit is the framework's signal-to-noise ratio, defined as the squared Mahalanobis norm of the noiseless signal:

$$ \mathrm{SNR}(P)^2 \;:=\; J(P, \emptyset, \Sigma) \;=\; \mu_P^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mu_P. $$

For a unit-norm $U_p$ in white noise, this reduces to $\mathrm{SNR}(P) = F/\sigma_{\hat F}$ where $\sigma_{\hat F}$ is the matched-filter retrieval std on a single source — flux divided by the noise on the retrieved flux. The dimensionless ratio

$$ R \;:=\; \mathrm{SNR}(P)\,/\,\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}} $$

lets us read off detectability at a glance: at $R \geq 1$ the configuration is bright enough to clear the threshold.

Calibrating $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}$

The threshold is fixed by requiring that noise alone fail to support a single-source model at the 5σ level (Dannert et al. 2022, Cacaj et al. 2025 §2.2.3):

$$ \mathbb{P}\!\left[\,\mathrm{S/N}\bigl(Q_1^\star(X), \Sigma\bigr) \,>\, \eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}\,\right] \;=\; \Phi(5) \;\approx\; 1 - 5.7\!\times\!10^{-7}, $$

with $X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$ pure noise and $Q_1^\star(X) = \arg\min_{|Q|=1} J(Q, X, \Sigma)$ the best matched-filter fit. Naively one might expect $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}} = 5$ — that's the 5σ tail of any single matched filter. But $Q_1^\star$ is the maximum over a continuum of positions $q$ in the FOV; taking a maximum over correlated Gaussian samples shifts the bulk of the distribution upward (the “look-elsewhere effect” in particle physics). So $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}$ deviates from 5, by an amount that depends on the FOV size, the PSF correlation length, and the parametric Gaussian-fit approximation used to extrapolate the tail.

The threshold is computed numerically: simulate many noise-only data, find $Q_1^\star$ for each, build the histogram of the maximum matched-filter $\mathrm{S/N}$, fit a Gaussian, and read off the 5σ point of the fit ($\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}} = \mu_{\mathrm{fit}} + 5\sigma_{\mathrm{fit}}$). The next demo illustrates this for the 2D Gaussian PSF used by the early demos on this page; in the $\delta_1$-for-an-aperture-telescope demo below, the same MC is run once per aperture and the resulting $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}$ drives plot 9's region classification.

Three conditions for contamination

A two-source configuration $P = \{p_1, p_2\}$ is contaminating when the matched-filter $Q^\star$ wins the total-cost contest against $\emptyset$, $\{p_1\}$, $\{p_2\}$ and the truth $P$ itself. Three necessary-and-sufficient conditions (Cacaj 2025 Eqs. 7–9, with $\alpha = \beta = 1$) decompose this requirement, with $\eta$ playing the role of $\eta_{\mathrm{SNR}}$ from the previous section:

  1. Detect. $J(P, \emptyset) - J(P, Q^\star) > \eta^2$ — the data is far enough from "no source" to detect something.
  2. Resolve fails. $J(P, Q^\star) > \eta^2$ — the single-source fit $Q^\star$ does not reach the data within tolerance, so the data cannot be explained by one source alone.
  3. Contaminate. $\displaystyle \min_{P_s \in \{\emptyset, p_1, p_2\}} J(P_s, Q^\star) > \eta^2$ — even the simplest competitors ($\emptyset$, the brighter source, the fainter source) sit further from $Q^\star$ than $\eta^2$, so the phantom $Q^\star$ wins out.

The target confusion map

Conditions (1)–(3) collapse into the sign of a single noise-independent quantity:

$$ \mathcal{D}(P) \;=\; \min_{P_s \in \{\emptyset,\, p_1,\, p_2\}} J(P_s, Q^\star) \;-\; J(P, Q^\star). $$

The sign reads off contamination at every noise level:

  • $\mathcal{D}(P) > 0$ — the geometry admits a phantom $Q^\star$ that beats every simpler hypothesis. Contamination is geometrically possible: for low enough noise the data will prefer the phantom over the truth.
  • $\mathcal{D}(P) < 0$ — no such phantom exists; contamination is ruled out at every noise level.

Normalising by source intensities,

$$ \bar{\mathcal{D}}(P) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{D}(P)}{2 J_1} \;+\; \frac{\mathcal{D}(P)}{2 J_2}, \qquad J_i = F_i^2, $$

gives a flux-scale-invariant diagnostic that lies in $[-1, 1]$ for the smooth PSF dictionaries of physical interest.

When is $\bar{\mathcal{D}}$ bounded?

The bound $|\bar{\mathcal{D}}| \leq 1$ is not automatic — it can fail for pathological discrete dictionaries. A clean sufficient condition is the finite-correlation condition: with $c_i = \langle U_{q^\star}, U_{p_i}\rangle$, $r = \langle U_{p_1}, U_{p_2}\rangle$, and the convention $F_1 \geq F_2$,

$$ F_1\,(c_1 c_2 - r) \;\leq\; F_2\,(1 - c_2^2). \tag{FCC} $$

Whenever (FCC) holds, $|\mathcal{D}(P)| \leq \min(J_1, J_2)$ and $\bar{\mathcal{D}}(P) \in [-1, 1]$. For the isotropic Gaussian PSF in any dimension, (FCC) is satisfied unconditionally. That is the case the example below makes interactive, with the full proof.

The spatial resolution $\delta_1$

$\mathcal{D}$ classifies one configuration at a time. To turn the framework into a single number that characterises an instrument, fix a source $p_1$ and let a companion source $p_2$ sweep the field of view with fixed flux $F_2$. The configurations that contaminate $p_1$ form the contamination set

$$ \mathcal{C}(p_1, F_2, \Sigma) \;:=\; \bigl\{\, p_2 \in \Theta \;:\; \mathcal{D}(\{p_1, p_2\}, \Sigma) > 0 \,\bigr\}. $$

Its area, normalised so that a disk has area equal to its radius squared, defines the confusion area; its square root is the $\delta_1$ resolution length:

$$ \rho(p_1, F_2, \Sigma) \;:=\; \frac{1}{\pi}\!\iint_{\Theta} \mathbb{1}\{p_2 \in \mathcal{C}\}\; \mathrm{d}^2 p_2, \qquad \delta_1 \;:=\; \sqrt{\rho}. $$

The $1/\pi$ normalisation is chosen so that whenever the contamination set is a disk of radius $r$ around $p_1$, $\delta_1 = r$ exactly. This is the regime of every isotropic monotone radial PSF: the dimension-independence theorem then collapses $\delta_1$ to the 1D critical separation $\Delta_c$ along the segment $[p_1, p_2]$.

$\delta_1$ for an aperture telescope

For a circular pupil of diameter $d$ at wavelength $\lambda$, the unit response is the 2D Airy disk

$$ U(\theta) \;\propto\; \biggl[\frac{2 J_1(\pi\, \rho_r\, d/\lambda)} {\pi\, \rho_r\, d/\lambda}\biggr]^2, \qquad \rho_r = \|\theta - \theta_p\|, $$

where $J_1$ is the Bessel function of the first kind of order one and the first zero $\pi\, \rho_r\, d/\lambda = 3.8317$ defines the Rayleigh angle $\delta_{\mathrm{Rayleigh}} \approx 1.22\,\lambda/d$. The Airy PSF is isotropic but not monotone radial — the Bessel sidelobes break the monotonicity assumption used in the Gaussian dimension-independence proof. The red contour in the right-hand plot is the function $\delta_C(F_{P_1}/F_{P_2})$ — the smallest separation at which $\bar{\mathcal{D}}$ first changes sign. At equal flux it lands at

$$ \delta_1 \;=\; \delta_C \;\approx\; 0.952\,\delta_{\mathrm{Rayleigh}} \;\approx\; 1.16\,\frac{\lambda}{d}, $$

slightly tighter than the Rayleigh convention $1.22\,\lambda/d$. The constant $0.952$ is obtained by Monte Carlo integration of $\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{D} > 0\}$ over $\theta_{p_2}$ in the FOV (≤ 1% relative error after ~10 iterations; Cacaj et al. 2025, App. C). No closed-form expression for $0.952$ is known. Bracketed below by the Duval–Peyré empirical two-spike threshold $0.5$–$0.7\,\lambda/d$ and above by the Candès–Fernandez-Granda sufficient bound $1.87\,\lambda/d$, the value is order-optimal in a precise sense (Cacaj 2025, Theorem on order optimality).
