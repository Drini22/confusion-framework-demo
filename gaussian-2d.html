Theory: from $\mathcal{D}$ to $\Delta_c$ in $d$ dimensions

The interactive panels above are governed by three results that together fix the contamination threshold of the isotropic 2D Gaussian PSF — and indeed of every monotone radial PSF in any dimension. We present them in the order they're used.

Theorem 1 Gaussian PSF satisfies the FCC

Let $\sigma > 0$ and let $\{U_\theta\}_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d}$ be the unit-norm isotropic $d$-dimensional Gaussian shift dictionary, with autocorrelation

$$ k(t) = \langle U_\theta, U_{\theta + t}\rangle_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^d)} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\|t\|^2}{4\sigma^2}\right). $$

Let $p_1 \neq p_2 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $F_1 \geq F_2 > 0$, and let $q^\star \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be the matched-filter optimum $\arg\max_q\, F_1\,k(q-p_1) + F_2\,k(q-p_2)$. Set $c_1 = k(q^\star - p_1)$, $c_2 = k(q^\star - p_2)$, $r = k(p_2 - p_1)$. Then the finite-correlation condition

$$ F_1\,(c_1 c_2 - r) \;\leq\; F_2\,(1 - c_2^2) $$

holds with strict inequality whenever $p_1 \neq p_2$. Consequently $|\mathcal{D}(P, \Sigma)| \leq \min(J_1, J_2)$ and $\bar{\mathcal{D}}(P, \Sigma) \in [-1, 1]$ for every two-source configuration.

Proof

Reduction to 1D. The matched-filter gradient identity $\nabla_q k(q-p_i) = -(q - p_i)/(2\sigma^2)\, k(q-p_i)$ turns the first-order condition $\nabla f(q^\star) = 0$ into

$$ F_1\, c_1\, (q^\star - p_1) + F_2\, c_2\, (q^\star - p_2) = 0, \quad\text{hence}\quad q^\star = \frac{F_1 c_1 \,p_1 + F_2 c_2 \,p_2}{F_1 c_1 + F_2 c_2}. $$

So $q^\star$ lies on the segment $[p_1, p_2]$ — the entire problem collapses to a 1D analysis along that segment. With $u := \|q^\star - p_1\|$ and $v := \|p_2 - q^\star\|$, the projected FOC reads $F_1\, u\, c_1 = F_2\, v\, c_2$.

Reducing the FCC. Writing $\gamma = F_2 / F_1 = u c_1 / (v c_2)$ and using the explicit Gaussian form $c_1 = e^{-u^2/(4\sigma^2)}$, $c_2 = e^{-v^2/(4\sigma^2)}$, $r = e^{-(u+v)^2/(4\sigma^2)}$, the FCC collapses (after dividing by $c_1 > 0$) to

$$ 1 - e^{-uv/(2\sigma^2)} \;\leq\; \frac{u}{v}\,\bigl(e^{v^2/(2\sigma^2)} - 1\bigr). $$

Set $x := uv/(2\sigma^2)$, $y := v^2/(2\sigma^2)$ so $u/v = x/y$. The inequality becomes $1 - e^{-x} \leq (x/y)(e^y - 1)$, which is the product of two elementary tangent-line inequalities for $e^z$ around $z = 0$ — namely $1 - e^{-x} \leq x$ and $e^y - 1 \geq y$ — both strict when $x, y > 0$. Multiplying back gives the displayed FCC. $\blacksquare$

Theorem 2 1D Gaussian equal-flux threshold

For two equal-flux Gaussian sources at separation $\Delta$ in 1D, there is a unique critical separation

$$ \Delta_c \;=\; 2\sigma\,(1 + x_c)\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/x_c)}{1 - x_c^2}} \;\approx\; 2.83691\,\sigma, $$

where $x_c \approx 0.7643801282$ is the unique root of

$$ 2\bigl[x^{(1+x^2)/(1-x^2)} - x^{(1+x)/(1-x)}\bigr] + 2x^{2/(1-x^2)} - 1 = 0, $$

such that $\operatorname{sgn}\mathcal{D}(P) = \operatorname{sgn}(\Delta_c - \Delta)$. Below $\Delta_c$ contamination is geometrically possible; above it the two sources are resolved.

Theorem 3 Dimension independence of $\Delta_c$

Let $d \geq 1$ and let $\{U_\theta\}_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d}$ be a unit-norm shift-invariant dictionary with isotropic autocorrelation $\langle U_\theta, U_\eta\rangle = k(\|\theta - \eta\|)$, where $k$ is smooth, $k(0) = 1$, and $k'(r) < 0$ on the relevant range. For two sources of fluxes $F_1 \geq F_2 > 0$ at separation $\boldsymbol{\delta} := p_2 - p_1$, $\Delta := \|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|$:

  1. The matched-filter optimum $q^\star$ lies on the segment $[p_1, p_2]$.
  2. Parametrising $q^\star$ as $u = \|q^\star - p_1\|$, $v = \|q^\star - p_2\|$ with $u + v = \Delta$ and $\gamma = F_2/F_1$, the boundary $\mathcal{D}(\Delta) = 0$ is the dimension-free system $$ k'(u) = \gamma\, k'(v), \qquad 2[k(u)k(v) - k(u+v)] + \gamma[2k(v)^2 - 1] = 0. $$
  3. The threshold $\Delta_c$ — and the equal-flux $\delta_1 := \Delta_c|_{\gamma = 1}$ — is the same in every dimension. In particular, the volume metric $$ \delta_1^{(d)} := \left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_d\{\boldsymbol{\delta} : \mathcal{D}(P_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}) > 0\}}{\mathrm{Vol}_d(B_1)}\right)^{1/d} $$ equals $\Delta_c$ for all $d \geq 1$.
Proof sketch

(i) The gradient $\nabla f(q) = F_1 k'(\|q-p_1\|)\, \widehat{q-p_1} + F_2 k'(\|q-p_2\|)\, \widehat{q-p_2}$ is zero only when the two unit vectors are collinear (since both scalar coefficients are nonzero by $F_i > 0$, $k'(r) < 0$). So $q^\star$, $p_1$, $p_2$ are colinear.

(ii) Choose coordinates with $p_1 = 0$, $p_2 = \Delta\,e_1$, $q^\star = u\, e_1$. Strict monotonicity forces $u \in [0, \Delta]$. The FOC projected on $e_1$ is $k'(u) = \gamma\, k'(v)$. The boundary $\mathcal{D} = 0$ comes from specialising the algebraic identity $J(\{p_1\}, Q^\star) - J(P, Q^\star) = F_2[2 F_1(c_1 c_2 - r) + F_2(2 c_2^2 - 1)]$ to the case at hand.

(iii) The system in (ii) is two scalar equations in two scalar unknowns $u, v$; the ambient dimension never appears, so $\Delta_c$ is dimension-invariant.

(iv) By isotropy $\mathcal{D}$ depends only on $\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|$; the contamination region is $B_{\Delta_c}$, of volume $\mathrm{Vol}_d(B_1)\Delta_c^d$. Substituting yields $\delta_1^{(d)} = (\Delta_c^d)^{1/d} = \Delta_c$. $\blacksquare$

Combining the three. Theorem 1 guarantees the FCC for the Gaussian in any $d$, hence $|\bar{\mathcal{D}}| \leq 1$ universally. Theorem 2 pins down the 1D threshold at $\approx 2.83691\,\sigma$. Theorem 3 lifts that threshold verbatim into 2D, 3D, or any higher dimension — with the contamination region always a Euclidean ball of radius $\Delta_c$ around either source.

The yellow vertical line in the $\mathcal{D}$ plot above sits at $\Delta_c \approx 2.84\,\sigma$. Drag the sources past it and the curve goes green (no contamination); drop them inside and the curve goes red.
